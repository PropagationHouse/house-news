The Distribution Problem Nobody Talks About

Everyone's building apps. Almost nobody's thinking about how they reach people. A walkthrough of Glasser's web-first distribution model and why it changes the unit economics of software.

For the last fifteen years, the app store model has been treated as inevitable — a natural law of software distribution, like gravity. You build the thing, you submit it to the store, you give up thirty percent of your revenue, and in exchange you get a shelf in the world's biggest mall. Except the mall takes a cut of every sale, controls your listing, can delist you without explanation, and changes the rules whenever it wants. Glasser was the first project where we actually ran the numbers on what that shelf costs versus what it gives. The answer was uncomfortable.
