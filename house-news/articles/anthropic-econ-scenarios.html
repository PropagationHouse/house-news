Three Futures, One Model

Anthropic’s Economic Index ran three futures through the same model: business as usual lands US unemployment at 3.9 percent by 2030, a heavy AI adoption scenario reaches 11.9 percent. The spread between those two numbers is the whole AI-labor debate rendered as arithmetic — and it comes from the company whose product does the displacing.

By Propagation House·Friday, September 11, 2026·8 min read

Three modeled futures, one model. Photograph by house.

Anthropic’s Economic Index published "scenarios" on September 9: three modeled futures of AI and the US labor market through 2030, all run through the same model. In the baseline, unemployment drifts to 3.9 percent. In the heavy-adoption future, it reaches 11.9 percent — a level the last century hit exactly four times: 1930, 1931, 1932, 1933. The eight-point spread between the scenarios is the entire AI-labor debate rendered as arithmetic, published by the company whose product does the displacing.

The method is the headline. The Economic Index works from millions of real usage records — actual Claude conversations, mapped to occupations and tasks, weighted by employment. The scenarios project those usage patterns onto the labor market under three adoption paths: baseline (3.9 percent by 2030), heavy adoption (11.9 percent), and an acceleration case in between. Same model, same data, different adoption assumptions. The output is not a forecast — it is a conditional: what the number does if the adoption curve does X.

The 11.9 percent figure lands differently because of its echo. Unemployment has touched 11.9 percent in the modern era exactly four times — 1930, 1931, 1932, 1933. Anthropic’s scenario doesn’t predict a second Depression; it maps a heavy-adoption path onto labor-market arithmetic and notes that the endpoint of that path has a name. The publication is explicit that scenarios are not predictions, that adoption itself is uncertain, and that this is a tool for policymakers to reason with rather than a projection to react to.

The conflict is structural and worth stating plainly: Anthropic publishes the most granular public data on AI’s labor impact while selling the product that generates it. The index is credible because the data is real and the method is public. It is also marketing — being the company that quantifies the displacement is a position, not a neutral act. Both things are true. The useful move is to read the conditional, not the brand.

The Conditional Decade

The scenarios convert an argument into a dial: adoption speed is the only variable that separates 3.9 from 11.9. That is the report’s real finding — the difference between the futures is not the technology, it is the rate at which it gets deployed. Policy can move that rate. So can pricing, and DeepSeek just moved it (same edition, cover story). The conditional decade has a dial on it, and the dial is now the story.

The Takeaways

  • Anthropic’s Economic Index published three scenarios September 9 projecting US unemployment in 2030: 3.9 percent baseline, 11.9 percent under heavy AI adoption, and an acceleration case in between.
  • The scenarios are conditionals, not forecasts: same usage-based model, same data, different adoption-path assumptions - the spread is the adoption rate, not the technology.
  • 11.9 percent is not an arbitrary endpoint: unemployment has reached that level in the modern era only in 1930-33.
  • Anthropic publishes the most granular public data on AI labor displacement while selling the product that causes it - the data is credible and the publisher is interested; both facts matter.
