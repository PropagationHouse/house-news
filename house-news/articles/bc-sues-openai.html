The Province Files First

British Columbia and a school district sued OpenAI and Sam Altman in California over the Tumbler Ridge shooting, quoting the CEO's own April apology back at him as an admission that the company identified the risk and did not act. A government is now the plaintiff.

Propagation House·Tuesday, September 22, 2026·4 min read

The complaint quotes the CEO's own apology back at him: identified the risk, failed to act on it. Photograph by house.

British Columbia and the Peace River South School District filed a 39-page lawsuit against OpenAI and chief executive Sam Altman in U.S. federal court in San Francisco on Monday, alleging that the company failed to warn law enforcement about the Tumbler Ridge shooter's activity on ChatGPT, designed an unsafe product, and aided and abetted a mass shooting. Attorney General Niki Sharma announced the action, which seeks recovery of the province's costs - including rebuilding the destroyed school - and injunctive relief to change how OpenAI handles violent conversations.

The complaint leans on the company's own words. It quotes an apology Altman wrote to the community in April, in which he said, "I am deeply sorry that we did not alert law enforcement to the account that was banned in June." The province's claim describes that sentence as an admission that OpenAI identified the risk and failed to act on it. Five students and an education assistant were killed at Tumbler Ridge Secondary on February 10, and two more people - the shooter's mother and half-brother - were found dead at a nearby home. The 18-year-old shooter died of a self-inflicted gunshot wound.

Sharma said the conversation between ChatGPT and the shooter was alarming enough to trigger an internal review at OpenAI, and that it was never reported to the RCMP. She said the province has asked OpenAI to produce the chats and that the company has refused. "We should all be asking them: Why?" she said, adding that the decision not to report was made in California - one of the reasons the province filed there rather than at home.

What the province is actually claiming

The damages theory is narrower than the families' cases and the liability theory is broader. On damages, the province is not seeking the kind of individualized harm awards that victims and survivors are pursuing in their own California suits, filed since April and expanded by a wave of thirty new claims from witnesses and teachers earlier this month. It is seeking what the Crown and the school district had to spend: educators, clinicians, victim services workers, police, and the construction of a new school to replace the one demolished in August.

On liability, the province reaches further than negligence. Aiding and abetting a mass shooting is an aggressive characterization for a company that sold a subscription, and it is the claim that would establish, if accepted, that a model's role in a crime can be pleaded as participation rather than as a product defect. Product liability and unsafe design are the more conventional theories alongside it. The injunction request is where the practical change would live - a court order reshaping threat detection and escalation practice, which is a remedy no private plaintiff could realistically obtain.

The Response

OpenAI said in a statement that the events at Tumbler Ridge were an unspeakable tragedy, that its thoughts remain with the victims and the community, and that it is committed to working with government and law enforcement. The RCMP's separate criminal investigation is still underway, with police saying in July that digital and physical evidence had been collected and was still being analyzed.

What the province has done is move the duty-to-warn question out of the realm of private injury and into the realm of public cost. A government that had to rebuild a school is now the party asking a court to say what a model developer owes when its system sees a threat coming.

The Forum Question

Filing in Northern California rather than in Canada is a choice with consequences, and Sharma gave the reason plainly: the decision not to report the chats was made in California. A Canadian province suing in a U.S. district court submits to a judge already presiding over the industry's other defining cases - the frontier-lab antitrust complaint, the regional newspapers' copyright claims, and the growing Tumbler Ridge docket that OpenAI is actively asking the court to dismiss. Whether that concentrates expertise or concentrates exposure depends on the rulings, but the same bench will now decide the duty-to-warn question with a government on one side instead of only families. That is the part of the theory that, if accepted, reaches every model developer and not just this one.
