The Bridge

Fundamentals of 3D Spatial Design. A working theory of shared virtual space — hubs, halls, and havens — written from the practice of building them, from a parkour park in Bellingham to the UN General Assembly Hall.

By Michael L. BlackstonePropagation HouseFirst edition · 2024

Space is not a backdrop. It is the medium through which people meet, and the shape of a room decides what can happen inside it. The Bridge is the studio's working theory of that medium — written over three years of building spatial environments for retail, diplomacy, and play, and published in 2024.

The book opens with the practice: rooftops in Ohio, bar shifts that funded a Vive Pro, a $200,000 parkour park built with the city of Bellingham — shared space as common ground, manifested twice, once in concrete and once in virtuality. From there it builds the fundamentals: purpose, tone, shapes, scale, cohesion, light, sound.

“The higher I could climb, the safer I felt.”

Hubs, Halls & Havens

At the center of the book is the H³ framework — the studio's taxonomy for shared space:

  • Hubs — social gravity. Rooms designed for density, where the crowd is the content.
  • Halls — intermediary and temporary. Event space: built for a moment, dismantled after.
  • Havens — specialized landmarks. Small, persistent, personal — the room someone returns to.

The framework generalizes without strain, from VRChat lobbies to city planning — because the physics of gathering don't change when the room does.

Everything is Language

The closing chapters look forward: thought-spaces as a new asset class, value accruing through time spent and attention given, and the arrival of AI thought partners — digital ghosts that pick up exactly where you left off. Written in 2023–2024, the final chapters read today as a spec that has since been built.
