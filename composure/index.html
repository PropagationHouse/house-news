The Engine

Channels are not tracks.
Voices are.

A track is a container for events — a row in a spreadsheet of time. A channel is a voice: a persistent identity you can address, modify, or replace without disturbing the others. The difference between editing a document and having a conversation.

The Channel Map

Five fixed voices — Drums, Lead, Melody, Ambience, Bass — each running the full duration of the piece, each independently addressable. Ask it to compose and it returns five parallel streams, not one blob of MIDI.

The Mood Primitive

A parameterized emotional frame for generation. Not a preset, not a genre tag — a first-class object the engine composes against. Tell it how the room should feel; it figures out what the players do.

Agent-Native Surface

Sixteen tools over a JSON-RPC endpoint — channel ops, playback, sessions, mood. Built so an LLM can drive the whole instrument the way a producer drives a band: by talking to the parts.

Modify One, the Rest Hold

Say “transpose the bass down a fifth” and only channel 5 changes. No regeneration, no re-arrangement, no collateral drift. The drums, the lead, the melody, the ambience hold. That is the whole point: a song that can be edited the way a conversation is edited — one utterance at a time, with everything else staying itself.

get_channel_map

Shows the five voices and what each is doing right now

play_midi

Starts the voices — the whole band, live, at once

modify_playback

Mutates one channel without touching the other four

set_mood

Audio In, MIDI Out

Hum it, play it, record it. On-device pitch-to-MIDI turns anything you can make a sound into something the channel map can hold, edit, and hand back to you.

The Tablet as Controller

The Android build is the sketchbook in your bag — and a remote surface for the desktop engine. Capture the idea where it happens; finish it where the speakers are.

Sessions, Not Files

Every take is a session — chords, key, tempo, tags, notes, and the events themselves. Link them, loop them, vault them. A sketchbook with a memory.

Inside

The map,
drawn out.

“You don’t tell Composure what to play. You tell it what each player should be doing, and it figures out how they fit together.”

The distinction matters more than it sounds. A track is a container for events. A channel is a voice — a persistent identity that runs through the entire piece and can be addressed, modified, or replaced without disturbing the others.

That is the difference between editing a document and having a conversation. Composure is built for the second one.

A session mid-modification: the channel map on the left shows the five voices; the modify_playback call on the right transposes only channel 5 — the bass — without regenerating any of the other four.

No re-render. No drift. The song stays itself while one voice shifts — which is exactly how musicians have always worked, and exactly how no timeline editor lets you.

The Sketchbook

The studio in your bag,
the stage on your desk.

Composure refuses to choose between capture and performance. The same engine runs as a desktop instrument and an Android sketchbook — and the tablet can drive the desktop rig.

Hum it in, keep it forever

Voice-to-MIDI on device. A melody caught in a hallway becomes an editable voice in the channel map — no cable, no interface, no ceremony.

Drums, synths, and the vault

A step-sequenced drum forge, a synth generator, a knowledge graph of every session you’ve ever kept. The vault remembers what you forgot you wrote.

An agent can sit in the band

The JSON-RPC surface means Substrate — or any agent — can compose, mutate, and perform alongside you. The instrument was designed to be played by more than hands.
