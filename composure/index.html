Composure

A MIDI engine with a
collaborator inside.

Composure models a song as sixteen role-labeled channels on one virtual device, not as a timeline of tracks. Four carry default roles — Drums, Bass, Chords, Melody. The other twelve are open. Address one voice and the others hold.

It is two things at once, and it is worth saying both plainly. On Android it is a sonic sketchbook you carry. On desktop and Android together it is an agentic collaborator for production and recording — an instrument built from the first file to be played by something that is not you.

The Spine

Channels are not tracks.
Voices are.

A track is a container for events — a row in a spreadsheet of time. A channel is a voice: a persistent identity you can address, modify, or replace without disturbing the others. The difference between editing a document and having a conversation.

The Channel Map

Sixteen channels on one device, each holding a role rather than an instrument. Four default roles; twelve left open. Rename them, lock them against the agent, or hand one to a plugin and one to hardware. The map is yours.

The Mood Primitive

A mood is not a preset. It reframes the register the whole band plays in — scale, voicing, density, tempo feel — from a single keyword. Fourteen of them ship, from Warm to Dreamlike, and the agent can call them.

Modify One, the Rest Hold

Transpose the bass, scale the tempo, swap a voice — the engine transforms, it never regenerates. No re-render, no collateral drift. The song stays itself while one voice shifts. This is also what makes it safe to hand to an agent.

The Collaborator

An agent at the keys.
Two ways in.

Composure is agent-native in both directions, and this is the thing most software gets wrong. There are two separate agent surfaces, and they reach the same engine — one from outside the app, one from inside it.

Outside the app

The MCP surface

A Model Context Protocol server ships with the desktop app, speaking JSON-RPC 2.0 over Streamable HTTP on localhost port 9473. Any MCP client can drive it — including Substrate, which registers Composure as a connected tool the moment you switch it on.

play_midi modify_playback update_channel_map soundscape drum_forge synth_forge create_mood + 9 more

Sixteen tools in all. It can list sessions, load one, mutate a running performance, rewrite the channel map, program a drum pattern, build a synth patch, or author a mood — and it reports back what it did.

Inside the app

The in-app agent

There is also an agent panel built into the interface, wired to the same twelve core operations as the outside surface. It can read the current view, act on it, and play the instrument in front of you — no external client, no second window.

playMidi modifyPlayback updateSoundscape updateSynthForge updateDrumForge + 7 more

The signature move is the same on both sides: modify_playback. An agent edits the performance while it is happening — not a file, not a clip, the thing you are hearing.

“An agent that can only read the song is an assistant. An agent that can change one voice without disturbing the others is a bandmate.”

Dungeon Master

A quest engine that gives the collaborator a brief — a progression to write, a difficulty, a skill to practice. It turns practice into a prompt and the agent into a scene partner.

Brainstorm

Describe the idea in words and it exists: a MIDI part, a drum kit, a patch. The prompt is the sketch, and the sketch becomes playable.

Transform, never regenerate

Every agent action is surgical by design. Change the bass, keep the drums. The architecture enforces it, so you never gamble the good parts to fix the bad one.

In Your DAW

It reaches the hardware.

On desktop, Composure presents itself as a real virtual MIDI device. Your DAW sees it the way it sees any other instrument — because it is one.

A virtual instrument, named Composure

Notes, control change, pitch bend — routed straight into Ableton, FL Studio, Maschine, Logic, any DAW. Play from the app; hear it on the DAW's instruments.

Clock sync, both directions

MIDI Clock at 24 PPQ with start, stop, and continue. It can be your DAW's clock master, or follow it. Transport stays locked either way.

Sustain and expression aware

CC64 sustain, expression, and per-finger MPE when the mode is on: a note per finger, each on its own channel, pitch bend following the touch. A pro-controller feature, built in.

Underneath it, real DSP

The desktop build ships a native C++ audio engine over PortAudio, bound to the app through N-API. Not a Web Audio toy — a synthesis engine with a C++ core the interface talks to.

It Writes It Down

MIDI becomes
sheet music.

The Composer view engraves notation from scratch — not a library, not a plugin. Two thousand two hundred lines of hand-written scoring code, drawing to canvas and giving musicians something they can actually read.

Real engraving, not a piano roll

Key signatures, sharp and flat spelling, courtesy accidentals, tied notes across barlines, beam groups, 8va and 8vb for notes that leave the staff. Treble and bass clefs on one canvas.

For musicians and producers

Hand a session to someone who reads music and they can play it. Export the staff as a clean PNG for a chart, a lead sheet, a session note, dark or light.

Hum it in, keep it forever

Voice-to-MIDI runs on device through Spotify's Basic Pitch, resampled to 22.05 kHz and transcribed locally. Nothing is uploaded. A melody caught in a hallway becomes an editable voice in the channel map — and then notation.

It Generates

Sound you can
shape, not just record.

Two rooms do the heavy lifting here, and both are playable by hand or by agent.

Generative MIDI, routed to your channels

Soundscapes writes to the channel map, with per-track channel routing you set. Point the pad at channel 5 and it plays your synth. Drone, arpeggio, strum, pulse, random, percussion, melody — across fourteen moods from Warm to Dreamlike.

Chladni plates and native synthesis

Synth Forge is a full synthesis workbench — oscillators, filters, envelopes, an FX chain — with a visualizer that renders cymatic standing-wave patterns as you tune. Patches can be designed by hand or described to the agent.

Custom synths from your own audio

Record a sample from the mic or the system bus — or drop in a file — then trim it and build an instrument around it. The sampler transposes across the keyboard; the sample becomes a patch, and the patch becomes a voice.

Fourteen moods

Warm Dark Mystic Deep Calm Sad Tense Sweet Cinematic Ethereal Cosmic Meditative Joy Dreamlike

A mood is the single keyword the whole band answers to. Set one and the scale, voicing, density, and feel all move together — then keep playing. The agent can set it too, mid-performance.

On Android

The studio in your bag,
the stage on your desk.

The Android build is not a companion viewer. It runs the instrument, and it doubles as a controller for the desktop.

Standalone, or as a controller

The whole instrument runs on the device. Put it on the same network as the desktop and it also becomes a control surface — play from the tablet, hear it through the desktop, over WebSocket with an HTTP fallback. Sessions, audio, and patches sync between them.

Per-finger expression and haptics

Turn on MPE and each finger gets its own channel, with pitch bend and CC74 following the touch. Pads and keys answer with haptic feedback through the Capacitor Haptics plugin, so playing is felt as well as heard.

Capture anywhere

Sketch, hum, tap, and file it — then finish it at the desk. The archive is the same on both ends, so an idea caught on the bus is waiting when you sit down.

The Archive

Sessions, not files.
The archive is the instrument.

Every take is a session — chords, key, tempo, tags, notes, and the events themselves. Not a folder of exports; a searchable body of work.

The Vault

Sessions, patches, and audio in one archive — search, download, delete. Everything you make is kept and findable, not lost to a naming convention.

The Knowledge Graph

Sessions, ideas, and relations drawn as a map. The archive stops being a list and becomes something you navigate.

Sketch & Brainstorm

Draw a melody line and the playhead traces it; describe an idea and it becomes a part. Two low-friction doors into the same engine, for when a keyboard is the wrong tool.

Honest Notes

What it isn't.

An earlier version of this page overclaimed in four places. They are corrected here, on the page, because an instrument you can trust is worth more than one that sounds perfect.

There is no Bluetooth MIDI.

The app uses Web MIDI, so it will accept a keyboard your operating system has already paired over Bluetooth — but Composure is not doing the Bluetooth work. The tablet-to-desktop link is over your local network, not BLE.

Drum Forge models analog, not physics.

The earlier page called it “physical-model synthesis, not samples.” It is analog-style synthesis built on Tone.js oscillators, and the app also ships a sample engine. Good sounds; the wrong word.

“Scribe — Always Listening” was a slogan, not a sensor.

The pulsing red dot in the sidebar is a static element with no state behind it. The real, useful feature is a setting: launch on startup and capture MIDI without being asked. That is worth having; the dot is decoration.

The Obsidian export is not built yet.

Settings shows an Obsidian vault-path field. It has no handler and nothing writes a markdown file. The intent is real; the feature is not implemented, so it is off the page until it is.

Room count, corrected.

The page used to say thirteen rooms. The app has eleven in the sidebar and fourteen reachable views in its state model. Pick the number you can defend: this page uses eleven in the nav, fourteen in the app.

Five honest limitations cost less trust than one discovered overstatement.
