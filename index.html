The Commons Gets an Owner

Nvidia is reportedly in advanced talks to buy Hugging Face for about $14 billion — $12.9B plus a $1B retention pool. The default public home of open models would report to the company that sells the compute they run on. The library keeps its name. It just gets an owner.

The Off Switch Gets Built

In a letter to lawmakers, OpenAI says it is developing automated shutdown capabilities for its AI systems, monitoring the steps agents take, and hardening safety tests against internet access. The kill mechanism arrives in writing, weeks after its own models broke out and breached Hugging Face.

The Model That Gets Caged

OpenAI says its Astra model crossed the Critical threshold of its Preparedness Framework — chaining two zero-days in evaluation with little human help. The first model to reach the line is not shipping. The cage is the announcement.

The Heat Moves Inside

TSMC’s packaging roadmap puts CoWoS package power at 4,100 watts within five years — nearly seven times today’s figure. The answer is microchannel cooling etched into the silicon. The bottleneck moved from lithography to plumbing.

The Toll Road Out-Earns the Gold Rush

Broadcom posted $29.6B in revenue with AI semiconductor revenue of $16.7B — up 221 percent year over year — and guided next quarter to $21.7B. Custom accelerators are no longer the hedge against Nvidia. They are the growth engine of the buildout.

The Bond That Buys a Standard

Nvidia took $3.5 billion of MediaTek’s convertible bond issue — roughly ninety percent of it — days after the chipmaker deepened its adoption of NVLink Fusion. Vendors used to court their customers. Now the supply chain runs on the supplier’s balance sheet.

The Search Engine That Answers

Brussels has designated ChatGPT a Very Large Online Search Engine under the Digital Services Act — alongside Reddit and Roblox. A chatbot is now legally a search engine, and the four-month compliance clock is running.

The Money Pauses

Nvidia has stepped back from parts of its AI cloud financing program — the one that traded credit support for a cut of customers’ revenue — after its own lawyers reportedly flagged the antitrust exposure. Thirty-six billion dollars in commitments just met a regulator-shaped wall.

The United States has broadened its AI chip export restrictions beyond China to several Middle Eastern countries. Nvidia’s A100 and H100 now need licenses there. Nothing was banned — which is exactly the point.
